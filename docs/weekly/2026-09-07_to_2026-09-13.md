## 1. Technological Uncertainty

Block 7 established that the direct two-channel wearable detector has low event precision and that transfer effects vary across participant partitions. It did not establish whether the frozen detector relies on both wearable channels or whether a missing channel produces a stable, bounded change.

The first Block 8 experiment tested channel feature-contribution robustness while avoiding the confounding effect of retraining a new one-channel model.

## 2. Hypothesis and Method

The protocol was committed before result-producing implementation. It defined a material adverse change as either an event-F1 loss of at least 0.03 or a false-alarm increase of at least 0.50/hour relative to the intact detector.

The frozen `H2-D` model, scaler, threshold 0.96, validation assignment, support, and event-matching rule remained unchanged. For each channel ablation, all 40 corresponding context dimensions were replaced by their train-fitted `StandardScaler.mean_`, making their standardized contribution zero. The two fixed ablations were evaluated once against the intact model.

This method tests classifier-input contribution. It does not simulate a disconnected electrode, amplifier saturation, impedance noise, or correlated raw-signal artefacts.

## 3. Result

| Comparator | Precision | Recall | F1 | False alarms/hour |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| `H2-INTACT` | 0.0645 | 0.4324 | 0.1123 | 1.4558 |
| `H2-NO-HB1` | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0.5208 |
| `H2-NO-HB2` | 0.0549 | 0.2703 | 0.0913 | 1.0793 |

Neutralizing `HB_1` reduced F1 by 0.1123, exceeding the predefined material bound, and eliminated all true detections. Neutralizing `HB_2` reduced F1 by 0.0210, which remained inside the material bound. Both ablations reduced false alarms, showing that channel removal changed the operating behavior rather than simply adding false detections.

The overall screen failed because the detector was not robust to `HB_1` neutralization.

## 4. Participant Influence

Both comparisons retained their aggregate F1 and false-alarm directions in all 16 leave-one-`pid`-out folds. The `HB_1` F1 effect ranged from -0.1311 to -0.0916; the `HB_2` effect ranged from -0.0364 to -0.0105. The channel asymmetry was not caused by one validation participant.

The four highest-burden participants contributed 51.7% of intact false alarms, 75.9% after `HB_1` neutralization, and 45.9% after `HB_2` neutralization. This concentration remains a limitation even though the effect directions were leave-one-participant-out stable.

## 5. Validation and Reproducibility

- The frozen model SHA-256 matched before loading.
- Intact probabilities reproduced the prior Block 7 values within `1.12e-16`.
- All four intact event summaries reproduced within `1e-12`.
- All 15/15 in-run checks passed and the immutable rerun produced no change.
- The independent validator reconstructed 57,369 probabilities, all event outputs, 32 leave-one-participant-out folds, and false-alarm concentration.
- All 11/11 independent checks passed twice.
- No raw EDF or test artifact was accessed.

## 6. Failure and Knowledge Gained

The known two-channel method failed the predefined single-channel robustness screen. The failure is asymmetric: at the fixed threshold, `HB_1` feature contribution was necessary for any true detection, whereas removing `HB_2` caused a smaller but consistent loss.

The result does not justify simply dropping a channel, lowering the threshold, or retraining on the same validation result. It shows that representation and calibration effects are coupled under channel loss and need to be separated experimentally.

## 7. Next Decision

1. predeclare controlled raw-signal degradation levels and a no-refit validation evaluation;
2. distinguish score-distribution shift from event-ranking loss under degradation;
3. preserve the current model and threshold as fixed comparators;
4. keep the current test partition closed; and
5. require a new protocol before any channel-aware model or recalibration method.
