## 1. Technological Uncertainty

Block 7 established that the direct two-channel wearable detector has low event precision and that transfer effects vary across participant partitions. It did not establish whether the frozen detector relies on both wearable channels or whether a missing channel produces a stable, bounded change.

The first Block 8 experiment tested channel feature-contribution robustness while avoiding the confounding effect of retraining a new one-channel model.

## 2. Hypothesis and Method

The protocol was committed before result-producing implementation. It defined a material adverse change as either an event-F1 loss of at least 0.03 or a false-alarm increase of at least 0.50/hour relative to the intact detector.

The frozen `H2-D` model, scaler, threshold 0.96, validation assignment, support, and event-matching rule remained unchanged. For each channel ablation, all 40 corresponding context dimensions were replaced by their train-fitted `StandardScaler.mean_`, making their standardized contribution zero. The two fixed ablations were evaluated once against the intact model.

This method tests classifier-input contribution. It does not simulate a disconnected electrode, amplifier saturation, impedance noise, or correlated raw-signal artefacts.

## 3. Result

| Comparator | Precision | Recall | F1 | False alarms/hour |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| `H2-INTACT` | 0.0645 | 0.4324 | 0.1123 | 1.4558 |
| `H2-NO-HB1` | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0.5208 |
| `H2-NO-HB2` | 0.0549 | 0.2703 | 0.0913 | 1.0793 |

Neutralizing `HB_1` reduced F1 by 0.1123, exceeding the predefined material bound, and eliminated all true detections. Neutralizing `HB_2` reduced F1 by 0.0210, which remained inside the material bound. Both ablations reduced false alarms, showing that channel removal changed the operating behavior rather than simply adding false detections.

The overall screen failed because the detector was not robust to `HB_1` neutralization.

## 4. Participant Influence

Both comparisons retained their aggregate F1 and false-alarm directions in all 16 leave-one-`pid`-out folds. The `HB_1` F1 effect ranged from -0.1311 to -0.0916; the `HB_2` effect ranged from -0.0364 to -0.0105. The channel asymmetry was not caused by one validation participant.

The four highest-burden participants contributed 51.7% of intact false alarms, 75.9% after `HB_1` neutralization, and 45.9% after `HB_2` neutralization. This concentration remains a limitation even though the effect directions were leave-one-participant-out stable.

- All four intact event summaries reproduced within `1e-12`.
- All 15/15 in-run checks passed and the immutable rerun produced no change.

## 6. Failure and Knowledge Gained

The known two-channel method failed the predefined single-channel robustness screen. The failure is asymmetric: at the fixed threshold, `HB_1` feature contribution was necessary for any true detection, whereas removing `HB_2` caused a smaller but consistent loss.

The result does not justify simply dropping a channel, lowering the threshold, or retraining on the same validation result. It shows that representation and calibration effects are coupled under channel loss and need to be separated experimentally.

## 7. Next Decision

1. predeclare controlled raw-signal degradation levels and a no-refit validation evaluation;
2. distinguish score-distribution shift from event-ranking loss under degradation;
3. preserve the current model and threshold as fixed comparators;
4. keep the current test partition closed; and
5. require a new protocol before any channel-aware model or recalibration method.

## 8. Controlled Raw-Signal Noise Experiment

A second protocol was committed before its result-producing implementation. It tested whether the frozen `H2-D` detector degrades gradually under deterministic raw-rate Gaussian noise and whether the prior channel asymmetry persists at signal level. No model, scaler, threshold, split, label, or matching rule was changed.

For every validation recording and channel, one seeded 256 Hz standard-normal sequence was propagated through the frozen filter and resampling path. The same sequence was reused at nested 20, 10, and 0 dB levels. Noise was calibrated over samples in valid scored epochs. Five degraded conditions and the clean comparator were scored together without inspecting intermediate event outcomes.

## 9. Noise Result

| Comparator | Precision | Recall | F1 | False alarms/hour |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| `H2-CLEAN` | 0.0645 | 0.4324 | 0.1123 | 1.4558 |
| `H2-BOTH-20DB` | 0.0924 | 0.2973 | 0.1410 | 0.6777 |
| `H2-BOTH-10DB` | 0.1250 | 0.1351 | 0.1299 | 0.2196 |
| `H2-BOTH-0DB` | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0.0126 |
| `H2-HB1-10DB` | 0.0220 | 0.4324 | 0.0419 | 4.4616 |
| `H2-HB2-10DB` | 0.0667 | 0.1081 | 0.0825 | 0.3514 |

The 20 dB condition passed the predefined mild-noise screen. Its F1 difference was +0.0287 with paired participant interval -0.0362 to +0.0869, so the apparent improvement is inconclusive. Its false-alarm difference was -0.7781/hour with interval -1.5069 to -0.3322/hour.

The 0 dB condition detected no true event. Its F1 loss was 0.1123 with paired interval 0.0410 to 0.1869. This is a clear severe-noise failure even though false alarms also fell.

## 10. Dose and Channel Findings

Both-channel probability correlation with clean scores decreased from 0.8404 to 0.7276 to 0.4982 as SNR worsened from 20 to 10 to 0 dB. Mean absolute probability difference increased from 0.0705 to 0.1260 to 0.1640. The nested probability-dose hypothesis was supported.

At 10 dB, isolated `HB_1` noise reduced F1 by 0.0704 and increased false alarms by 3.0058/hour; both participant intervals excluded zero. Isolated `HB_2` noise reduced F1 by 0.0298, with an interval crossing zero, and reduced false alarms by 1.1044/hour. The larger `HB_1` F1 loss and probability displacement support the predeclared channel-asymmetry hypothesis.

The median score moved upward by 0.2417 under `HB_1` noise and downward by 0.0415 under `HB_2` noise. This opposite response identifies a channel-specific calibration mechanism that the missing-feature experiment alone could not show.

- All 17/17 in-run checks passed.

## 12. Failure and Knowledge Gained

The frozen detector is not uniformly noise robust. Severe both-channel noise suppresses detections, while moderate isolated `HB_1` noise produces a distinct high-score, high-false-alarm failure. Moderate isolated `HB_2` noise instead lowers sensitivity and alarm rate. This establishes that degradation type and channel identity interact with the fixed score calibration.

White Gaussian noise does not reproduce natural movement, impedance changes, clipping, shared-reference interference, or nonstationary electrode failure. The result is validation evidence from a reused cohort, not independent confirmation.

## 13. Updated Decision

No threshold revision is authorized. Any channel-aware masking or train-only noise-augmentation method must have a separate committed protocol, a stated mechanism, and a fixed comparison with the unchanged detector. The current test partition remains closed to method development.

## 14. Train-Only Noise-Augmentation Experiment

Following the raw-signal failure analysis, a separate protocol froze one mechanism-specific adaptation before implementation. The `H2-NA` model retained the original feature representation and balanced logistic classifier but trained on five fixed versions of each eligible candidate: clean, both-channel 20 dB, both-channel 10 dB, isolated `HB_1` 10 dB, and isolated `HB_2` 10 dB.

The 82 train recordings produced 328 noisy feature arrays. All 492 SNR calibrations passed. The 2,743 base labeled candidates produced exactly 13,715 augmented rows. Five participant-grouped train folds generated 75,539 clean full-night held-out scores and selected threshold 0.99. Train-OOF F1 was 0.1193 with 1.2169 false alarms/hour. The final model and threshold were frozen before validation access.

## 16. Participant Interpretation

The isolated `HB_1` false-alarm reduction was consistent across the paired participant bootstrap: -5.8546 to -1.6828/hour. Its F1 improvement interval, -0.0129 to +0.1167, crossed zero. Clean F1 and FAR differences also had intervals crossing zero.

The method increased false alarms relative to `H2-D` under both-channel 20 dB, both-channel 10 dB, and isolated `HB_2` degradation, with intervals excluding zero. It therefore corrected a specific failure but did not provide uniform robustness.

## 18. Cross-Modality Event-Overlap Analysis

A separate protocol was committed before calculating whether the frozen direct PSG and wearable models detected the same validation references. No model, feature array, raw signal, or full probability table was opened, and no threshold was selected.

At the primary +/-15-second endpoint, the 37 reference events separated into 13 detected by both direct PSG and direct wearable, seven detected by PSG only, three detected by wearable only, and 14 missed by all three direct models. Six-channel PSG recall was 0.5135, direct wearable recall was 0.4324, and two-channel PSG recall was 0.2703.

The non-deployable union of the three direct-model detections reached recall 0.6216, 0.1081 above the best individual direct model. This is a recoverability diagnostic only because a combined false-alarm burden was not calculated.

## 22. Fusion Decisions and Uncertainty

Both OR methods clearly failed. For `P6-H2-OR`, the F1-difference interval was -0.1072 to -0.0244 and the FAR-difference interval was +0.7536 to +1.6837/hour. Complementary event hits therefore did not overcome accumulated alarms.

The 2-of-3 consensus gate passed at the point estimate: F1 improved by 0.0543 and FAR fell by 0.5710/hour relative to `P6-D`. The F1-difference interval, -0.0192 to +0.1323, crossed zero; the FAR interval, -1.1759 to -0.1346/hour, did not. The result supports false-alarm suppression more strongly than improved event detection.

The consensus F1 increase at +/-45 seconds was +0.0363 with interval +0.0059 to +0.0754. Its direction was consistent but it failed the fixed +0.05 materiality gate.

## 23. Fusion Validation and Decision

All 12 source-model metric rows reproduced. Every source alarm entered each applicable clustering exactly once, all cluster spans were at most 30 seconds, and all consensus alarms had at least two model contributors. All 10/10 in-run and 10/10 independent checks passed. An immutable rerun reproduced every reviewed output. No model fitting, threshold search, probability access, or current-test access occurred.

The 2-of-3 rule depends on simultaneous PSG and wearable recording and remains low-precision. It is retained as a laboratory mechanism comparator, not as a wearable result. Fixed alarm fusion stops at v0.1. The useful finding is that cross-view agreement suppresses false alarms; reproducing that specificity with wearable-only temporal evidence remains unresolved.
