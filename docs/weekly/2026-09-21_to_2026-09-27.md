## 1. Technological Uncertainty

The eight-epoch wearable logistic detector represented temporal context as 80 unrelated columns and remained low precision. Cross-view consensus had previously shown that structured agreement can suppress false alarms, but required PSG. The unresolved question was whether wearable-only learned temporal ordering and an explicit event-state sequence could reproduce that specificity mechanism.

## 2. Frozen Method

The protocol was committed on September 19 before result-producing implementation. It fixed:

- one small bidirectional LSTM plus three-state linear-chain CRF;
- the same 2,743 reviewed train candidates and stored wearable bandpower features used by the direct baseline;
- a matched flattened logistic comparator;
- the five existing participant-grouped train folds;
- 60 fixed training epochs without early stopping or search;
- full-night out-of-fold event scoring and separate 99-point thresholds;
- a required F1 gain of at least 0.05 with no false-alarm increase; and
- complete validation/test closure.

## 3. Execution Failure and Correction

The first completed run was rejected when an over-broad path check matched `validation` in the name of the historical train-feature directory. Its full outputs were retained. The predicate was narrowed to explicit validation/test path segments; no input, model, threshold, metric, or hypothesis changed.

The corrected run was accepted on September 22. All external fold models and full-night scores reproduced their stored hashes.

## 7. Decision

`LC-1` is eligible for a new locked or external wearable confirmation protocol. It does not replace a frozen model and must not be evaluated on the already used validation or current test partitions. F1 remains below 0.20 in this train-development estimate, so the result is promising method evidence rather than application readiness.

The combined comparison cannot isolate the LSTM contribution from the CRF contribution. No retrospective ablation is added. Block 9 external-PSG compatibility remains the next scheduled block and is not a wearable confirmation cohort.

## 8. Nested Architecture Exploration

A second protocol froze four small temporal candidates before execution: the prior bidirectional LSTM-CRF, a bidirectional GRU-CRF, a three-block dilated TCN-CRF, and a bidirectional LSTM with separate pre-boundary and post-boundary heads. The TCN receptive-field specification was corrected from two to three blocks before implementation or result generation so that it covered the complete eight-epoch context.

The comparison used nested participant-grouped evaluation. Candidate and threshold selection occurred only in four rotating inner folds, followed by one-time scoring of each closed outer fold. All candidates received the same input, training budget, class weighting, and event evaluator.

## 9. Nested Result

The selected pipeline reached F1 0.1645 and 0.2812 false alarms/hour, versus 0.1604 and 0.2971/hour for the matched nested BLSTM-CRF. Both pipelines detected 32/180 reference events; selection removed ten false positives without adding a true positive.

The F1 difference was +0.0041, with participant interval -0.0532 to +0.0608. The false-alarm difference was -0.0159/hour, with interval -0.0621 to +0.0296/hour. The required +0.05 F1 gain was not reached, and neither favorable direction was participant-supported.

Architecture choice was unstable: BLSTM-2H, BLSTM-CRF, BGRU-CRF, BGRU-CRF, and TCN-CRF were selected across the five outer folds. This fails to support a stable advantage for a deeper or alternative encoder on the current eight-epoch bandpower representation.

## 11. Error Diagnostic

On September 23, a retrospective diagnostic used only frozen train outer-fold outputs. No model was fitted and validation/test artifacts remained closed. The analysis separated timing, threshold transfer, quality-tier dependence, and labeled-transition proximity.

Widening tolerance from +/-15 to +/-45 seconds raised true detections from 32 to 41 and F1 from 0.1645 to 0.2113. Widening further to +/-105 seconds added only one event. Timing uncertainty is real but does not explain most missed events.

Post-result threshold upper bounds reached F1 0.2177 with one pooled outer-label threshold and 0.2324 with a separate label-informed threshold per outer fold. These deliberately optimistic values are not reusable thresholds. They show that threshold transfer contributes to error while also bounding calibration-only improvement below F1 0.25 in this sample.

## 12. Quality-Tier Finding

All three train procedures detected primary MAD-flagged events more often than primary clean events at +/-15 seconds:

| Model | Clean recall | MAD-flagged recall |
|---|---:|---:|
| LR-OOF | 0.0566 | 0.4252 |
| LC-1 | 0.1698 | 0.3701 |
| Nested selected | 0.0189 | 0.2441 |

The 10-MAD flag is nonspecific, so this association is not interpreted as proof of artefact detection. It identifies an unresolved dependence on amplitude, participant composition, or correlated recording properties that aggregate F1 concealed.

Of 177 nested primary false alarms, 167 were not within 45 seconds of any labeled transition. Transition-type vetoes therefore cannot remove most errors.

## 13. Verification and Next Hypothesis

- The initial complete diagnostic lacking historical controls was preserved as an incomplete result.

A draft next experiment now isolates one mechanism: retain the exact LSTM-CRF architecture, replace participant-fit mean/standard-deviation scaling with median/interquartile-range scaling, and equalize total positive loss contribution between clean and MAD-flagged events. The proposed gate requires +0.05 overall F1, +0.10 clean-event recall, and no false-alarm increase. It remains a draft and was not executed.
