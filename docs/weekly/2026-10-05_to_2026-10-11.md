# Weekly Research Record: 2026-10-05 to 2026-10-11

**Work performed:** 2026-10-07 to 2026-10-11

**Dataset:** BOAS OpenNeuro `ds005555`, snapshot `1.1.1`

**Data boundary:** Train only; validation and current test remained closed

## 1. Technical uncertainty

The existing detector used 180 primary REM-to-Wake events and 2,563 review-sampled negatives. A pre-experiment audit regenerated 73,476 eligible full-risk-set negatives and showed that the review sample covered only 3.488%, occurred much earlier in the night, and had a materially different centre-stage-pair distribution.

It was uncertain whether omitted negative diversity or the approximately 210-fold inflation of the positive training prior under balanced loss explained the poor full-night performance.

## 2. Hypotheses and controls

A protocol was committed before result-producing implementation. Three candidates retained the same wearable signals, 360-value enriched context, participant folds, elastic-net model, regularization, thresholds, and event evaluator:

- `SAMP-BAL`: exact prior sampled-negative balanced-loss control;
- `RISK-BAL`: all eligible full-risk-set negatives with balanced loss; and
- `RISK-NAT`: the same full risk set with natural class prevalence.

The primary operating constraint was 0.25 false alarms/hour, with predeclared 0.10, 0.50, and 1.00/hour sensitivity budgets. Validation and current-test access were prohibited.

## 3. Work completed

- Regenerated the full train risk set and verified 73,656 labelled rows, including 180 positive events.
- Reused the exact reviewed control scores and froze five participant-grouped outer folds.
- Completed 40 new inner fits and 10 new outer-final fits.
- Selected fold-specific thresholds from inner out-of-fold scores only.
- Evaluated 75,539 supported outer boundaries per candidate.
- Computed event metrics, risk-set calibration metrics, and 2,000-resample participant-cluster intervals.
- Applied all four predeclared alarm budgets to the frozen outer scores without refitting.
- Added independent validators for the primary and alarm-budget result packages.

## 4. Primary result

At the primary +/-15-second tolerance and 0.25/hour selection budget:

| Candidate | Precision | Recall | F1 | False alarms/hour |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| SAMP-BAL | 0.0526 | 0.0500 | 0.0513 | 0.2574 |
| RISK-BAL | undefined | 0.0000 | 0.0000 | 0.0000 |
| RISK-NAT | 0.1480 | 0.1611 | 0.1543 | 0.2653 |

The negative-coverage hypothesis failed because `RISK-BAL` recall decreased by 0.05 relative to `SAMP-BAL`. The natural-prior hypothesis passed its point gate: Brier score fell from 0.035599 to 0.002708, a 92.39% reduction, while recall increased by 0.1611.

For `RISK-NAT - RISK-BAL`, participant-bootstrap intervals were `+0.1046` to `+0.2314` for recall, `+0.1043` to `+0.2057` for F1, and `+0.1873` to `+0.3552` false alarms/hour. Improved detection therefore came with increased alarm burden relative to the zero-alarm balanced formulation.

## 6. Failed approaches and corrections

1. The sequential implementation projected approximately nine hours of runtime. Independent fits were parallelized without changing model settings.
2. Thread-local warning capture was not isolated, so the first concurrent run misreported convergence. Those outputs were rejected and archived; convergence was then derived directly from iteration counts.
3. Balanced-loss models produced exact probabilities of 1.0, invalidating the assumed no-alarm threshold. A protocol amendment added the next representable value above 1.0 before outer evaluation.
4. The first sensitivity execution incorrectly expected 73,656 outer scores, confusing the labelled calibration risk set with all 75,539 supported prediction boundaries. It stopped before writing outputs; the corrected control derives coverage from the frozen support table.

## 7. Convergence limitation

Forty-four of 50 new fits reached 3,000 iterations. Twenty-four of 25 `RISK-BAL` fits and 20 of 25 `RISK-NAT` fits did not converge; only both fold-2 outer-final fits converged. The frozen settings were not changed. The natural-prior direction is retained, but coefficient and threshold stability require a predeclared numerical-stability control before it can serve as a Block 10 comparator.

## 10. Interval-aware comparison

A separate protocol and shared-preprocessing amendment were committed before result-producing code. A genuine 15-second feature grid was generated from raw wearable EEG rather than by interpolating the existing 30-second features. It contained 150,967 supported boundaries across the same 82 train recordings and 64 participant groups.

`HARD15` assigned each of 180 primary events to the nominal boundary. `INT15` assigned weight 1/3 to nominal -15, nominal, and nominal +15 seconds, preserving total positive event mass. Both candidates shared 147,055 negatives, pruning, scaling, regularization, participant folds, alarm budgets, and evaluation.

## 11. Interval-aware result

All 50 predeclared fits stopped before the iteration limit. At the primary +/-15-second tolerance and 0.25 false-alarms/hour budget, `HARD15` reached F1 0.0109 and 0.0032 false alarms/hour; `INT15` reached F1 0 and 0.0048 false alarms/hour. The F1 difference was -0.0109, and its participant-bootstrap interval was -0.0380 to 0. The +0.03 advancement gate failed.

The same result held across the four alarm budgets because each candidate generated only three outer alarms. The uniform interval-label mechanism therefore received the frozen decision `stop_v0.1`.

## 12. Post-run numerical audit

The iteration-count convergence control did not detect score degeneration. Fitted intercepts ranged from approximately -1005 to -749. Exact endpoint probabilities accounted for 99.87% of outer scores for both candidates: 150,763 exact zeros for `HARD15` and 150,746 for `INT15`, with six and 31 exact ones respectively.

This establishes a failed SGD configuration rather than a stable biological comparison. The frozen event tables and stop decision were not rewritten. A separate audit script and two reviewed numerical tables preserve the failure mode explicitly.
