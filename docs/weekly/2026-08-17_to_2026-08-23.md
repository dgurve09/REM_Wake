## 1. Technological Uncertainty

The stage-first SF-C model produced useful but modest sleep-stage performance while generating excessive REM-to-Wake false alarms through stage-sequence fragmentation. It remained uncertain whether a simple classifier trained directly on the target boundary could reduce that event-level failure using only simultaneously recorded wearable headband EEG features.

The work tested direct-boundary value, not general sleep staging and not clinical effectiveness.

## 2. Predeclared Method

`docs/evaluation/direct_event_baseline_protocol_v0.1.md` was written before direct-model fitting or direct test-score access. It froze:

- DE-A logistic regression using the previous and current 30-second epochs;
- DE-B logistic regression using eight epochs from -120 to +90 seconds;
- 180 primary train REM-to-Wake events and all 2,563 primary reviewed train backgrounds;
- train-only scaling and balanced class weighting without negative subsampling or hyperparameter search;
- full-night scoring at every supported 30-second boundary;
- one alarm per contiguous above-threshold run;
- 99 validation thresholds from 0.01 to 0.99;
- validation selection by event F1, then false alarms/hour, recall, and threshold;
- primary +/-15-second evaluation, prespecified quality and +/-45-second sensitivities, and participant bootstrap intervals.

The protocol recorded that the overall test partition had already been used for the planned stage-first comparison. Direct-model files and thresholds were still isolated and hash-frozen before direct test-feature access.

## 3. Train and Validation Result

Both models retained all requested labeled rows. DE-A used 20 inputs and converged in 53 iterations; DE-B used 80 inputs and converged in 106 iterations. Neither emitted a convergence warning.

Validation window discrimination improved with context: average precision was 0.4544 for DE-B versus 0.3913 for DE-A. That improvement did not translate into the prespecified event endpoint:

| Model | Selected threshold | Precision | Recall | Event F1 | False alarms/hour |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| DE-A | 0.97 | 0.0714 | 0.2973 | 0.1152 | 0.8909 |
| DE-B | 0.96 | 0.0648 | 0.4324 | 0.1127 | 1.4496 |

H6.2 was therefore not supported on validation. DE-B remained primary as predeclared rather than being replaced after seeing the result.

## 5. Frozen Test Result

The hash-locked models and validation thresholds were applied once to test feature arrays without refitting:

| Method | Precision | Recall | Event F1 | False alarms/hour |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| SF-A | 0.3333 | 0.4237 | 0.3731 | 0.2609 |
| SF-C | 0.0438 | 0.3051 | 0.0766 | 1.9692 |
| DE-A | 0.0625 | 0.2203 | 0.0974 | 0.9703 |
| DE-B | 0.0909 | 0.4237 | 0.1497 | 1.2571 |

DE-B met H6.1's directional criterion relative to SF-C. The paired participant bootstrap estimated an event-F1 difference of +0.0731 with a 95% interval of +0.0110 to +0.1659 and a false-alarm-rate difference of -0.7121 per hour with a 95% interval of -1.2709 to -0.2044.

The improvement does not establish usable detection. DE-B produced 25 true positives, 250 false positives, and 34 false negatives. Only nine of 20 test participants had a true positive, while 18 had at least one false positive. SF-A remains stronger descriptively but cannot serve as an independent trained comparator because its provenance is not established locally.

## 6. Post-Result Failure Analysis

The exploratory analysis was planned after the primary result and did not change it. Its first execution failed because some false-alarm recordings contained no human REM/Wake boundary, making nearest-boundary distance undefined. The failed attempt was retained, a `no_remwake_reference_in_recording` category was added, and the analysis reran without model or threshold access.

Of 250 DE-B false positives:

- 108 were human other-to-Wake pairs;
- 56 were human REM-to-other pairs;
- 76 had no human stage change;
- 10 were another human transition;
- zero were exact human REM-to-Wake centers left unmatched by the evaluator.

Partial-endpoint confusion accounted for 65.6% of false alarms. Only 17 false positives, 6.8%, occurred within 135 seconds of a REM/Wake boundary; 192 were beyond 135 seconds and 41 occurred in recordings without any REM/Wake reference. The excluded boundary zone is not the dominant observed failure source.

At +/-45 seconds, DE-B gained eight eligible matches beyond the primary tolerance: two were one epoch early and six one epoch late. Relative to DE-A, DE-B added 12 true positives and 55 false positives, increasing event F1 by 0.0523 but increasing false alarms by 0.2868 per hour on test.

## 7. Validation-Only Endpoint Factorization

Before changing the model, the train/validation background construction was audited for the two dominant partial-endpoint errors. The train set already contained 466 REM-to-other and 546 other-to-Wake reviewed backgrounds, and both categories covered all 64 train participants. The failure was therefore not explained by simple absence of hard-negative examples.

A sequential improvement protocol was frozen after the earlier test failure was known. DE-D retained the same 80 context features and the same 2,743 train rows as DE-B but fitted separate logistic heads for REM-before and Wake-after. Their probabilities were multiplied as a fixed conjunction. The protocol authorized train and validation only and explicitly prohibited current-test access.

Both heads converged without warnings. The selected validation threshold was 0.74:

| Model | Precision | Recall | Event F1 | False alarms/hour |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| DE-B | 0.0648 | 0.4324 | 0.1127 | 1.4496 |
| DE-D | 0.0971 | 0.4595 | 0.1604 | 0.9915 |

DE-D met the predeclared two-part improvement rule: event F1 increased by 0.0477 and false alarms decreased by 0.4581 per hour. Partial-endpoint validation false positives decreased from 151 to 110.

A post-result paired participant analysis found that false positives decreased for 11 of 16 validation participants, were unchanged for one, and increased for four. The paired bootstrap DE-D minus DE-B event-F1 interval was +0.0038 to +0.1008, while the false-alarm-rate interval was -0.8835 to -0.1423 per hour. True-positive counts increased for two participants, were unchanged for 12, and decreased for two, indicating that the principal gain was false-alarm reduction.

Threshold robustness was then tested from saved validation probabilities only. DE-D exceeded both DE-B comparison values at 22 adjacent thresholds from 0.67 through 0.88. A leave-one-participant-out analysis selected a threshold from 15 participants and applied it to the held-out participant. All 16 folds selected 0.74, so aggregated held-out F1 remained 0.1604 and false alarms remained 0.9915 per hour. The paired DE-D-LOPO versus DE-B bootstrap intervals remained +0.0038 to +0.1008 for F1 difference and -0.8835 to -0.1423 per hour for false-alarm-rate difference.

The first threshold validator stopped because it used bitwise DataFrame equality for a floating-point interval span after TSV serialization. The failed validation attempt was retained, numeric comparison was changed to a fixed tolerance without changing results, and the corrected validator passed 13/13 checks.

## 8. Endpoint Contribution Diagnostic

A result-blind post-result plan tested whether DE-D's validation gain required both endpoint heads or was carried mainly by the easier Wake-after target. No model was fitted. The analysis used the saved validation candidate probabilities and compared REM-only, Wake-only, and the unchanged product under the same 99-threshold event evaluator.

| Comparator | Selected threshold | Precision | Recall | Event F1 | False alarms/hour |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| DE-D-rem-only | 0.89 | 0.0357 | 0.2162 | 0.0613 | 1.3554 |
| DE-D-wake-only | 0.85 | 0.0319 | 0.6757 | 0.0609 | 4.7629 |
| DE-D-product | 0.74 | 0.0971 | 0.4595 | 0.1604 | 0.9915 |

The product reproduced the earlier DE-D result exactly and had both higher F1 and fewer false alarms per hour than either head alone. REM-only scoring produced 73 REM-to-other false alarms versus 35 for the product. Wake-only scoring produced 464 other-to-Wake false alarms versus 75 for the product. This supports the proposed two-endpoint mechanism on the used validation partition, while the independently selected thresholds and absence of new-cohort evaluation prevent a generalization claim.

This is promising method-development evidence, not an updated test result. DE-D was not applied to the current test partition and must be evaluated under a new lock or external cohort.

## 9. Reproducibility and Gate Decision

The baseline validator passed 22/22 checks, including exact alarm and metric recomputation, phase isolation, support accounting, 99 thresholds per model, two model hashes, and all 128 reused feature hashes. The DE-B diagnostic validator passed 9/9 checks across 33 hashed inputs. DE-D passed 19/19 factorization checks, including endpoint-label reconstruction, probability-product verification, validation-only isolation, exact metric recomputation, and 102 reused feature hashes. Its paired participant analysis passed 6/6 checks, threshold robustness passed 13/13, and the endpoint-contribution diagnostic passed 7/7 in-run plus 11/11 independent checks across 26 hashed inputs. Raw EDF, model weights, full-night score artifacts, and feature arrays remain outside Git. Compact train/validation labeled-row score tables are retained for exact metric recomputation.

The Block 6 baseline gate remains closed with a decision to continue direct-event research while retaining the low-precision test limitation. A CNN remains deferred because the simpler factorized structure improved validation behavior, and the current test result must not be used for iterative model selection.
